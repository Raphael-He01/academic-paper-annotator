# Authored synthetic source — not a real study

## P001 — Abstract
This authored demonstration contains 200 synthetic reports. It illustrates why higher error detection does not necessarily mean better overall review. No patients, readers, or trained models were studied.

## P002 — Methods
The synthetic dataset has 40 reports with errors and 160 reports without errors. The two conditions are constructed rules applied to the same reports, rather than independent human reading experiments.

## P003 — Results
Rule A flags 26 of the 40 error reports and 8 of the 160 correct reports. Rule B flags 35 of the 40 error reports and 15 of the 160 correct reports.

## P004 — Results
Recall rises from 0.65 to 0.875, but the false-alert proportion also rises from 0.05 to 0.09375. The denominator for recall is 40, whereas the denominator for false alerts is 160.

## P005 — Discussion
A reviewer who only sees recall may prefer Rule B. That preference depends on the costs of missed errors and unnecessary alerts. These costs were not measured, so the demonstration does not establish which rule is more useful.

## P006 — Conclusion
The transferable lesson is to report both benefits and errors with their denominators. All counts can be regenerated from the accompanying CSV. The example is complete as an authored teaching fixture, not as evidence about a medical system.
